The reconstruction step follows a target-preservation logic. The main text states the operating conditions needed for interpretation; proof details and full notation are in Supplementary Notes 1 and 3. Three conditions matter for the reported operator: the local design separates direct lags from network exposure, reconstruction retains the separated coefficient blocks, and finite-horizon responses remain stable enough that coefficient error stays controlled after propagation. Whether low-rank reconstruction improves recovery relative to local estimation is assessed in the controlled benchmarks.

The inferential target is the finite-horizon response generated by a topology-indexed lag operator. For lag k and reported date t, the operator equals the direct coefficient block plus the network coefficient block multiplied by a supplied topology matrix:

$$
M_{k,t}(W)=A_{k,t}+B_{k,t}W .
$$

The need to retain the blocks can be stated as an endpoint-identifiability condition. Fix a fitted topology $W_0$ and let $D_{k,t}=A_{k,t}+B_{k,t}W_0$ be the collapsed map. Over an unrestricted matrix-block class, this map is non-injective: for any conformable perturbation $H$, the blocks $A_{k,t}^{H}=A_{k,t}-HW_0$ and $B_{k,t}^{H}=B_{k,t}+H$ produce the same $D_{k,t}$ at $W_0$, yet their operator at $W_1$ differs by $H(W_1-W_0)$. The collapsed map alone therefore does not identify direct-only or alternative-topology endpoints. A restricted block class can admit recovery only when an inverse from $(D_{k,t},W_0)$ to admissible $(A_{k,t},B_{k,t})$ is specified and identified. The collapsed benchmark ablation estimates no such inverse; its structural endpoints are outside the fitted target. Supplementary Note 1 gives the derivation and scope boundary.

Here $W$ is a supplied exposure matrix for response evaluation on a fixed fitted path. In the reported readouts, $W_t$ denotes the date-specific observed exposure matrix. The frozen-topology matrix $W_{pre}$ is the average of the predetermined exposure matrices over the 2016 Q1--2019 Q4 benchmark window, using quarterly import-share matrices for RCEP and rolling monthly mobility matrices for NYC Taxi. Topology laws of motion, memory kernels and nonlinear contagion rules are outside this response-evaluation argument.

The total response recursion evaluates this operator at the date-specific topology. The direct-only recursion evaluates it at zero network mediation, which leaves the direct coefficient block alone. The frozen-topology recursion evaluates it at the pre-period benchmark topology with the same coefficient blocks. The network-mediated propagation object is the finite-horizon difference between these recursions. This target is a defined model object when the design separates lagged direct regressors from lagged network-exposure regressors and when the exposure matrix is predetermined for the response recursion.

A local weak-separation diagnostic makes the design condition observable. For each rolling window and equation, the network-exposure regressors, including the lag-specific exposure blocks, are residualized on the direct lag regressors. The minimum eigenvalue of the residualized exposure Gram matrix reports how much independent variation remains for the network block. The 1e-8 flag used in the empirical tables is a numerical singularity screen within a fitted design. Small values mean that the direct/network split depends mainly on penalty and design choices. This is why the empirical analysis reports weak-separation, topology and stability diagnostics together.

The low-rank stage is interpretable when the local coefficient path is noisy and close to a low-dimensional temporal trajectory. It regularizes the separated coefficient tensor while retaining the block index needed for the reported operator. Its recovery advantage is an empirical question: the benchmark compares separated CP reconstruction with unrestricted local rolling estimation under declared endpoint and replication contracts.

Finite-horizon stability links coefficient recovery to response recovery. If the companion powers generated by the evaluated operators remain bounded over the reported horizon, perturbation bounds map block-level coefficient error continuously into response error. The same argument applies to observed, direct-only and frozen-topology recursions by evaluating the topology argument at $W_t$, zero or $W_{pre}$. Ratio summaries inherit this continuity when their denominators are bounded away from zero.

**Proposition 1 (finite-horizon response transfer).** Fix a reported date, a supplied topology matrix $W$ and a horizon $H$. Throughout, $\|\cdot\|$ is a compatible induced, hence submultiplicative matrix norm, and the fixed companion-state selection map $J$ and its transpose $J'$ have norm one. Let $\mathcal{C}(W)$ and $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(W)$ be the true and reconstructed companion matrices, and suppose their powers through $H$ have norm at most $K$. If the shared shock-normalization map has norm at most $L_{\Sigma}$, then

$$
\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\|\widehat{\Psi}_{h}(W)-\Psi_{h}(W)\right\|
\leq
L_{\Sigma}K^{2}\frac{H(H+1)}{2}
\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(W)-\mathcal{C}(W)\right\|.
$$

For the standard lag-$p$ companion construction, the final norm is bounded by $\sum_{k=1}^{p}\{\|\widehat{A}_{k,t}-A_{k,t}\|+\|W\|\|\widehat{B}_{k,t}-B_{k,t}\|\}$. The same statement applies at observed, zero-network and frozen-topology arguments. This proposition transfers a given separated-block error into a finite-horizon response bound. It does not establish a CP rank, CP-ALS global convergence or lower error than unrestricted local estimation. Supplementary Note 3 gives the proof; benchmark recovery results test the latter comparison.

Interpretability requires four conditions: the topology matrix is predetermined for the response recursion; the local design distinguishes direct lags from network exposure; reconstruction preserves separate direct and network blocks; and topology sensitivity is reported because perturbing the topology matrix changes exposure even when the coefficient path is unchanged. The synthetic missing-edge and noisy-weight scenarios, the frozen-topology comparison and the alternative-topology panel are designed around these conditions.
