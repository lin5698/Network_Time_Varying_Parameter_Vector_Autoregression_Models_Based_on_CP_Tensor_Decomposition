The controlled simulation tests the design principle on the computation it protects. Every method receives the same unrestricted local rolling estimates, and the reconstruction target is the only change. The collapsed ablation smooths the total map $\widehat A_t+\widehat B_tW_t$; CP and Tucker smooth the separated direct and network blocks. Across 20 replications at each of $N=15$ and $N=30$, the collapsed reconstruction matches CP on retrospective next-state error to within {{collapsed_next_state_gap_max}}%. On that fit criterion the two representations are indistinguishable. Under a supplied topology they are not: the collapsed object returns no frozen-topology or network-component response, because Proposition 1 shows that the total map does not determine them. These readouts are marked `OUTSIDE TARGET` before scoring (Fig. 3c).

The separated reconstructions return the topology-substitution response and recover it accurately. Relative to unrestricted local rolling estimation, CP reduced median frozen-topology response error by {{frozen_gain_n15}}% and {{frozen_gain_n30}}% at $N=15$ and $N=30$, and Tucker by {{tucker_frozen_gain_n15}}% and {{tucker_frozen_gain_n30}}%. Median network-component error under CP fell by {{netcomp_gain_n15}}% and {{netcomp_gain_n30}}%. On the observed-topology endpoints, CP reduced median effective-operator error by {{operator_gain_n15}}% and {{operator_gain_n30}}% (Fig. 3b) and median finite-horizon unit-shock response error, under the protocol normalization of operators with spectral norm above 0.95, by {{response_gain_n15}}% and {{response_gain_n30}}%. Each reduction is $100\times(m_{\mathrm{local}}-m_{\mathrm{CP}})/m_{\mathrm{local}}$, where $m$ denotes the across-replication median of the corresponding error metric in the same scenario.

The frozen-topology gain persists across the stress design. In all {{scenario_count_all}} scenarios, including rank misspecification, abrupt drift, heavy-tailed noise, high topology volatility, missing edges, noisy networks and $N=50$, CP reduced median frozen-topology response error by {{frozen_gain_min_all}}–{{frozen_gain_max_all}}%. The three topology-stress scenarios have 20 replications per method; the remaining stress rows have three, and $N=50$ has four per core method and one per sparse or projected graph-feature diagnostic. CP and Tucker perform similarly on the topology-substitution endpoints, so the evidence supports block separation with low-rank temporal sharing rather than a particular decomposition.

The primary design shares one low-rank coefficient path across matched topologies. A separate endpoint-aware experiment evaluates held-out recovery under a stricter native protocol: CP passed 0 of 16 required cells and Tucker passed 6 of 16; neither met the pre-specified criterion. Neither candidate passed any of the eight native-design cells (Supplementary Note 4). The recovery gains therefore apply to the matched design, while the certificate itself is estimator-independent.

Projected graph-feature comparisons use fixed mappings and are reported as supplementary diagnostics; the separately archived native-comparator output uses a different evaluation protocol.
