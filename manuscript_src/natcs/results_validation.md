Once endpoint availability has been established, the replicated controlled simulation asks whether preserving the separated operator improves recovery in its declared data-generating process. Across 20 replications at each of the primary $N=15$ and $N=30$ scales, the endpoint-preserving CP reconstruction reduced median effective-operator error by 93.6% and 96.8%, respectively, relative to unrestricted local rolling estimation (Fig. 3a). Median finite-horizon unit-shock response error decreased by 82.5% and 87.3% under the same comparison (Fig. 3b). Each percentage is computed as $100\times(m_{\mathrm{local}}-m_{\mathrm{CP}})/m_{\mathrm{local}}$, where $m$ is the across-replication median of the same scenario's effective-operator error or finite-horizon unit-shock response error, respectively. This supports only a bounded tested-endpoint statement within the declared synthetic DGP, comparator and replicated rows. It is not independent empirical validation, a fairness result or evidence of broad estimator superiority; the $N=50$ rows remain scaling stress checks rather than headline evidence.
