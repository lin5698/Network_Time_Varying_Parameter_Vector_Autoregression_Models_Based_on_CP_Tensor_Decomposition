Topology-dependent responses pass four distinct gates. The fitted object must first define the requested endpoint; the observed design must then identify the relevant direct and network actions; an estimator must recover those actions; and the resulting recursion must remain stable over the reported horizon. Figure 2 begins with the first gate. CP and Tucker reconstructions operate on the separated direct/network tensor, so both keep the declared topology-response endpoints defined. A collapsed total-map reconstruction defines total-map and total-GIRF outputs but no inverse to network-component or frozen-topology responses; it serves as a structural negative control, not a numerical recovery loser. Projected graph-feature diagnostics and their fixed mappings are reported in Supplementary Note 4 and Supplementary Tables 1b-3; they are not used as a main-text ranking of native graph-learning systems.

The next benchmark separates the preservation target from the CP smoother. Tucker-network reconstructs the same separated direct/network tensor and keeps the topology-substitution responses defined. It serves as a matched-rank preservation comparator under the shared endpoint contract. Across high-topology-volatility, missing-edge and noisy-weight settings, each with at least 20 replications for preservation-focused methods, CP-network has GIRF-error range 0.069-0.073 and frozen-topology error range 0.068-0.099. Tucker-network has GIRF-error range 0.146-0.148, frozen-topology error range 0.131-0.168 and instability range 48.5-55.1%, compared with 4.3-5.1% for CP-network. In these tests, both methods preserve the target; the stability diagnostic defines the finite-horizon boundary for reading response errors.

Stress-grid CP and Tucker rows use 20 replications where reported; local-rolling stress rows are diagnostic 3-replication anchors. A later endpoint-aware gate tested whether the favourable controlled-design result extends to native held-out response endpoints. Under the frozen N=20, T=200 protocol, CP passed 0 of 16 required cells and Tucker passed 6 of 16; Tucker passed none of the eight native-design cells, and no candidate was promoted. This negative gate separates formal endpoint availability from statistical recoverability. It prevents the original benchmark gains from supporting a broad endpoint-aware or native-recovery claim (Supplementary Note 4).
