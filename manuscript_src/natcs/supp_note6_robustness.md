This supplementary note reports the empirical robustness layer behind the RCEP application. The checks focus on benchmark dependence, clipping prevalence, stability monitoring, bootstrap design, network-measurement stress tests and network-structure diagnostics. The sign of the baseline pair-level association remains positive across import-based, export-based, symmetric and eight-quarter network definitions. The frozen-topology coefficient is 79.4% of the evolving-topology coefficient, with a conditional pair-cluster bootstrap interval of 74.3-87.5% for the ratio and 0.000022-0.000530 for the coefficient difference. This interval conditions on the reconstructed CP path and provides a second-stage joint uncertainty check. A 500-draw window-wise moving-block residual perturbation re-estimates the local and CP stages; its coefficient-difference interval is -0.000071 to 0.000470, while ratio draws remain a denominator-stability diagnostic when evolving-coefficient draws approach or cross zero. The two-way clustered sensitivity keeps the point estimate positive under the reported variance design, and the trimmed raw-metric sensitivity checks whether the descriptive association survives without the bounded regression transform. The top-exposure perturbation holds the CP-reconstructed coefficient path fixed while attenuating the date-specific highest import-exposure node; this checks sensitivity of the propagation object to a controlled topology perturbation.

The clipping diagnostic reports how often the bounded second-stage outcome is pinned at 0 or 1 and summarizes the raw distribution from which the clipped metric is derived. The aggregate propagation series also exhibits timing sensitivity. Structural-break diagnostics place the dominant aggregate breaks around 2021 Q2. These break dates summarize when the estimated propagation series changes most sharply in the regional trade panel.

Three interpretation constraints accompany the RCEP analysis. Quarterlyization may transmit measurement error from annual MRIO inputs into the quarterly propagation objects. The second-stage regression is a generated-regressor exercise and supports descriptive interpretation. Pandemic-era trade reorganization and lagged-network predeterminedness are plausible sources of benchmark dependence in the RCEP operator readout. The frozen/evolving ratio interval quantifies joint second-stage uncertainty around the two topology evaluations conditional on the reconstructed CP path. The stability diagnostics flag dates whose effective lag operator reaches the unit-radius boundary. Supplementary Table 4d reports the full-sample, stable-date-only and stability-projected values for the same pair-level coefficient, aggregate propagation index, frozen-topology aggregate index and observed-minus-frozen aggregate difference.

The added network-structure panel is a diagnostic interpretation layer. It relates aggregate propagation summaries to density, weighted concentration, node-strength inequality, exposure concentration, spectral radius, spectral gap, spectral effective rank, weighted reciprocity, directional asymmetry, weighted assortativity, a spectral bipartition diagnostic, edge turnover and weight turnover computed from the same baseline import-share matrices. In the reported evidence, density and edge turnover are constant or effectively non-informative because the baseline $W_t$ is dense after row normalization. The most visible association with the aggregate propagation index is spectral gap, followed by directional asymmetry, lower spectral effective rank and node/import-exposure inequality; weight concentration, turnover, assortativity and the bipartition diagnostic are weaker. The panel supports diagnostic interpretation of the propagation estimates and leaves causal claims about structural features to designs with exogenous topology variation.
