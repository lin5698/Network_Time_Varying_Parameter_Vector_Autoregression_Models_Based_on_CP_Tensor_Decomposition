The NYC Taxi panel is a public second-domain operator check. It supplies time-varying, predetermined topology outside the trade setting and uses the same reconstructed operator to compute aggregate propagation, frozen-topology contrasts and GIRF decompositions. The check tests computability in a public non-trade weighted network and the ability to return a near-null topology-substitution result.

We use the Manhattan yellow-taxi tensor released with the time-varying low-rank autoregression benchmark of Chen et al. [@chen2024; @xinychenvars2026]. The auxiliary panel keeps the 15 highest-flow mobility units and aggregates monthly log trip activity. It replaces bilateral trade weights with rolling 12-month trip-share networks built from the public trip tensor [@nyctlc2026]. The estimator uses a 40-month rolling local window, selects CP rank 1, and contains 69 post-window months.

The aggregate topology substitution is near zero under the same fitted-object definition. Supplementary topology summaries provide level-association context for the fitted aggregate series. The largest associations involve weight turnover, lower spectral effective rank and incoming-flow concentration (Supplementary Figure 10 and Supplementary Table 13). These descriptive correlations contextualize the fitted aggregate level. The retained monthly dates are inside the unit-radius stability region, with 0.0% unstable retained dates, so the public panel returns aggregate, frozen-topology and GIRF readouts under a stable retained-date path.

This second-domain result establishes public computability and the ability to return a near-null contrast. It does not establish recovery across topology families, network scales or data-generating regimes.
