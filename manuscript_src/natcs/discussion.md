The main finding is representational: temporal smoothing can produce a usable dynamic map while removing the inputs needed to define topology-dependent responses. Keeping direct dynamics, network transmission and topology separately evaluable retains the query. A collapsed map retains total responses but requires an additional identified inverse before it can define network-specific or alternative-topology responses.

This distinction extends a broader lesson from temporal-network research: stored representations determine which structures, dependencies and dynamics remain measurable. Dynamic-community models preserve state structure, memory representations preserve temporal dependence, sequential predictors preserve layer information and contagion learners preserve local dynamics. The present contribution concerns which response queries survive temporal reconstruction. Existing global, spatial and network autoregressions already permit changing exposure weights and response analysis; those operations are not new here.

The evidence also shows why endpoint definition and numerical recovery must remain separate. Separated CP reduced effective-operator and impulse-response error relative to unrestricted rolling estimation in the original controlled N=15 and N=30 design. A stricter endpoint-aware gate did not validate broad native recovery by the tested CP or Tucker candidates. The trade and mobility panels illustrate the readout; they do not rescue the estimator claim. RCEP remains a descriptive fixed-path comparison whose re-estimation interval includes zero, and the near-null New York City taxi contrast shows that an available topology comparison need not be substantively large.

Several conditions limit the conclusions. A defined endpoint may remain weakly identified when network exposures add little variation beyond direct lags. Even an identified endpoint may be poorly recovered, as the stricter native-design gate demonstrates. Finite-horizon responses also require a stable reconstructed path. The empirical intervals are pointwise and conditional on selected tuning settings. Contemporaneous network formation, omitted states and policy assignment make the reported contrasts descriptive and non-causal. The monitored spectral radii support the calculations but do not establish the bounded-operator assumption used in the deterministic response-transfer result.

In practice, the scientific query should be specified before coefficients are smoothed. Alternative-topology responses require a representation that retains or identifies the topology action, sufficient excitation to estimate that action and sufficient stability to propagate its error. These conditions should be reported separately, and a failed condition should not be hidden by returning an unqualified response. The present evidence establishes the representation distinction and fixed-path computability, not general native recovery or causal topology effects. A reusable estimator will require successful held-out recovery, calibrated uncertainty and validation across network families and scales.
