This supplementary note collects the operating regime, diagnostic failure modes and interpretation boundaries that are distributed across the main text.

The framework is designed for panels in which three conditions hold. First, the weighted network can be treated as a predetermined exposure object for the response recursion, at least after lagging or rolling construction. Second, the analyst has a substantive reason to distinguish direct persistence from lagged network exposure. Third, local coefficient estimates are noisy enough that temporal regularization is useful, while the underlying direct and network coefficient paths are sufficiently smooth or low-dimensional for CP reconstruction to be a plausible bias-variance device. When these conditions fail, the estimator can still be run, and the propagation interpretation requires additional sensitivity checks.

The main failure modes are diagnostic. Near-collinearity between lagged direct variables and lagged network exposures weakens the split between direct and network-mediated coefficient blocks. Effective companion operators close to the unit-radius boundary make finite-horizon amplitudes sensitive to small coefficient perturbations. Noisy observed topology makes the response sensitive to the measured exposure matrix. Coefficient paths far from a low-rank temporal trajectory can be oversmoothed or misallocated across factors by CP reconstruction.

These failure modes determine the evidence design. The synthetic benchmark includes rank misspecification, abrupt drift, high topology volatility, missing observed edges, noisy observed weights and heavy-tailed shocks. The empirical analysis reports alternative topology definitions, frozen-topology comparisons, clipping diagnostics, stability exclusion, stability projection and bootstrap block-length sensitivity. The NYC Taxi operator check tests whether the same operator responses run in a public mobility network with 69 post-window dates and 0.0% unstable retained dates.

The weak-separation diagnostic operationalizes the local design condition in Methods. For each rolling window and equation, the diagnostic residualizes the network-exposure regressors against the direct lag regressors and computes the minimum eigenvalue of the residualized network-exposure Gram matrix. In the RCEP panel, the median residualized minimum eigenvalue is 1.38e-4, the 1st percentile is 4.13e-5, the median condition number is 4.84, and 0.0% of rolling-window equation diagnostics fall below the 1e-8 numerical near-collinearity threshold. In the NYC Taxi operator check, the corresponding values are 1.02e-6, 3.16e-7, 50.18, and 0.0%. The absence of near-singularity flags indicates no near-singular windows at this threshold; separation strength is summarized by the reported eigenvalues and condition numbers.

The projected graph-feature stress tests examine the object-recovery question under controlled graph-feature fits. Sparse network TVP-VAR, graph-convolution VAR, graph-neural surrogate, diffusion graph VAR and recurrent graph-filter VAR rows measure how local graph structure performs after projection into the reported direct/network operator protocol and without block-preserving low-rank temporal reconstruction. The benchmark gives these rows matched 20-replication coverage at N=15, N=30 and in the three topology-stress scenarios, with N=50 retained as bounded stress output and row-level coverage reported in Supplementary Table 3. Prediction-only graph neural baselines require a separate held-out forecasting protocol and a topology-response mapping before their native outputs can be compared to these operator readouts.

The contribution centers on the estimand and the representation requirement. Standard TVP-VARs make coefficients time-varying. Spatial, network autoregressive and GVAR-style models encode cross-sectional exposure. Tensor decompositions stabilize high-dimensional coefficient arrays. Graph-learning models can be strong predictors. The present framework ties these ingredients to one requirement: the reconstructed path remains a topology-indexed operator, every reported propagation summary is computed from that operator, and every robustness check preserves or explicitly perturbs the same topology argument.

The interpretation sequence is as follows.

1. Verify that $W_t$ is lagged, predetermined or otherwise defensible as an exposure matrix for the response recursion.
2. Inspect whether direct lag regressors and network-exposure regressors have enough independent variation for the direct/network split to be interpretable.
3. Select rank and ridge penalty by blocked validation, and report the selected values and the candidate grid.
4. Monitor the spectral radius of the effective lag operator and qualify propagation amplitudes near or beyond the stability boundary.
5. Report at least one topology-construction sensitivity and one frozen-topology comparator when the empirical claim concerns evolving topology.
6. Separate operator-level, aggregate and GIRF claims from raw pair-level attribution claims, because the latter is empirically more fragile.
7. Treat second-stage regressions that use generated propagation summaries as descriptive unless the design supplies independent causal identification.
8. Distinguish propagation-object recovery from prediction-only performance when comparing with graph-learning or low-rank reduced-form predictors.

Under this sequence, endpoint-preserving low-rank reconstruction yields an operator-level measurement of time-varying network-mediated propagation in the evaluated operating regime.

The sequence ties all components to a single operator target. A rolling network VAR supplies local direct and exposure blocks; CP reconstruction supplies temporal regularization; and finite-horizon response analysis supplies propagation summaries. Every smoothing, tuning and robustness step preserves, projects to, or explicitly perturbs the topology-indexed operator $M_{k,t}(W)$. This keeps direct-only and frozen-topology responses defined.
